POINTS AND STROKES
points lists printed point names as strings. Use _1, _2, ... for unlabeled geometric junctions that must be referenced; these are temporary names, not printed labels. Reuse the same name at the same junction. Do not mistake a letter's position for the geometric point.
strokes lists {"kind":"segment","points":["A","H","B"]}. Points are ordered along ONE straight stroke: H lies between A and B. Use kind="line" or "ray" only when explicitly required. A dashed stroke can still be a segment. Include auxiliary lines and use the same intersection point in every participating stroke. Do not join unrelated strokes or treat every projected crossing in a 3D drawing as a junction.

CIRCLES AND ARCS
circles lists {"name":"c1","center":"O","through":["A","B"]}. Center is optional when unknown; through lists points on the circumference, not interior points. Use short local names for unnamed curves. Do not invent a center point.
arcs lists {"name":"a1","circle":"c1","ends":["A","B"],"through":["C"]}. Declare the supporting circle too. Through points distinguish which arc is meant. If the arc cannot be distinguished semantically, record the ambiguity instead of giving pixel hints.

REFERENCES
A point is referenced by its name. Other references describe geometry directly:
{"segment":["A","B"]}, {"line":["A","B"]}, {"ray":["A","B"]}, {"angle":["A","B","C"]}, {"polygon":["A","B","C"]}, {"circle":"c1"}, {"arc":"a1"}, {"region":"r1"}.
The middle point is the angle vertex. Polygon vertices are in boundary order. Code creates and deduplicates objects; do not assign IDs to individual segments or angles.

RELATIONS
Use {"type":...,"on":[references],"basis":"mark"}; basis is "diagram", "mark", or "text".
- parallel, perpendicular, equal_length, equal_angle, similar, congruent, tangent: two references. Example: {"type":"perpendicular","on":[{"segment":["A","H"]},{"segment":["B","C"]}],"basis":"mark"}.
- midpoint: [point, segment]. collinear: three or more points.
- point_on_segment, point_on_line, point_on_ray, point_on_circle, point_on_arc: [point, support]. Stroke point order and circle through already express membership; no repetition needed.
- rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, triangle: [polygon]. angle_bisector: [angle, ray starting at its vertex].
Matching ticks mean equal_length, matching arrows mean parallel, a right-angle square means perpendicular, and matching angle marks mean equal_angle. Emit relations, not descriptions of symbols. Connect every member of an equal-mark group using pairwise relations.
Follow the actual arms of a right angle. A foot lies on its support and connects to an altitude perpendicular to it; the diagonal containing the foot is not that altitude.
Keep circle membership and tangencies in structured statements. Never infer equal lengths, midpoints, or perpendicularity from visual proportions alone. Tangency needs a stated or clearly depicted contact. Question statements remain conditions even when the drawing is not to scale. Do not add derived theorems.

MEASUREMENTS, REGIONS, TARGET
measurements lists {"type":"length","on":{"segment":["A","B"]},"value":"x+21","unit":"cm","basis":"diagram"}. Types: length, radius, diameter, arc_length, angle, area, perimeter, ratio. Preserve expressions without solving, e.g. "sqrt(3)" or "2/3". Bind to the actual side/subsegment, angle, circle, arc, polygon, or region; distinguish radius from diameter and altitude from diagonal. Ratio uses an ordered pair of references in on: numerator then denominator. Basis="text" means the number is only stated in the question.
regions lists {"name":"r1","boundary":[references in boundary order],"holes":[],"shaded":true}. A polygon boundary may list vertex names. Holes reference separately named regions. Declare unshaded regions needed by the question too.
target uses {"type":"area","on":{"region":"r1"},"mode":"value"}. For a requested proposition use mode="proof" and its participating references; for an explicit requested comparison use mode="constraint". Ratio targets use an ordered pair of references. Keep an unknown symbol in an optional symbol field. A target is not a given measurement. Use null if no question or unknown is specified.
